# Gotchas

- **Publish with the transaction of the change.** Publish on the pool writes outside it: a rolled-back order still announces itself, and a failure between the ledger row and the job leaves a pending delivery without a job (`Redeliver` fixes it).
- **Handlers are idempotent and order-blind.** Delivery is at least once and unordered; key effects by the aggregate and drop updates with an older version.
- **Consume the queues, with room on `events.Queue`.** The worker must consume `events.Queue` (deliveries, registrations, sweep) and `events.SubscriberQueue(name)` (or every `SubscribeOptions.Queue`), otherwise nothing moves. jobs' default concurrency is 1: one subscriber whose endpoint hangs holds that slot for the client's timeout (30 s) per attempt, and every delivery behind it waits. Give `events.Queue` a concurrency (8 in the example) and the client a timeout that fits your subscribers.
- **Subscribe before Run, Register after the last Subscribe.** jobs refuses handler registration while the worker runs.
- **No authentication on the inbox, authentication on the registration.** The signature authenticates the inbox; the registration route needs the API key middleware and `Authorize`.
- **The default client refuses private networks.** Inside a cluster pass a safehttp client with `AllowPrivateNetworks` or `AllowedNetworks`; a refused address fails the delivery at once.
- **Disabled means skipped.** Events published while a subscription is disabled are not recorded and never delivered; only failed deliveries can be redelivered. A subscription an administrator disabled stays, disabled, when its subscriber drops it, so dropping and re-adding it does not lift the disable.
- **Rotating `InboxKey` needs a registration.** Until the publisher stored the new secrets, its deliveries are refused with 401 and retried; they do not count towards the auto-disable. A registration job is sent only by a replica with the inbox key that enqueued it.
- **Mid-rollout, any replica may send a registration.** The job carries the set of the replica that called `Register`, so an old replica sends the new set. Two registrations with different sets in flight at once (an older one still retrying while the publisher is down) end with whichever runs last; the next start registers again.
- **A subscription name is local to its service** (`create-invoice`); the job kind is `events.<service>.<name>`. Renaming a subscription is removing one and adding another: deliveries in flight for the old name are retried on the inbox's 404 until the publisher stored the new set, then recorded failed without counting. Changing a subscription's topic does the same to its pending deliveries of the old topic.
- **Events before the subscription are lost for it.** There is no event log in v0.
- **Events are small.** At most 32 KiB encoded (`MaximumEventBytes`), no NUL characters: carry references and a version, the subscriber fetches the rest.
